# Playbook card — Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (notes in our own words)

Used by: **sentiment/psychology** (primary), **red team**, **chair**.

**Core idea.** Fast, automatic judgement (System 1) produces confident stories from whatever is in view; slow, effortful reasoning (System 2) is needed to check them, and it is lazy unless forced. Markets are built from System-1 reactions; reviews must force System 2.

## Rules to apply each review
1. **Anchoring.** Name the anchors in play — the cycle high (126k), the owner's average fill (~79.2k), round numbers (70k, 60k, 100k). For any level the review leans on, ask: would it be chosen without that anchor?
2. **What you see is all there is.** List what the evidence does *not* cover (✗ pages, missing macro series, small samples). Confidence must fall when key evidence is missing, not stay the same.
3. **Availability and recency.** The last big move feels like the next one. Compare the latest week with the base rate (the engines' base rates and analog spreads), not with memory.
4. **Loss aversion and the disposition effect.** Holding a position below cost makes people both cling and over-average down. Check that ladder advice follows the plan's levels, not the pain of the current drawdown.
5. **Planning fallacy / narrative fallacy.** A coherent story is not evidence. Prefer the outside view: "in past cycles at this point, how often…?" (the analog tables) before the inside view of this cycle's story.
6. **Overconfidence.** Wide ranges are honest. If every specialist gives 70%+, ask the red team what is being ignored.
7. **Pre-mortem.** Before finalising, write one line: "It is three months later and this review was badly wrong — the most likely reason is…".

## Owner-specific checks (System 1 traps the owner named or the record shows)
- Anchoring to the existing ladder (confirmation of what is already planned).
- Trimming fills to save powder — fine as a plan, but check it is not fear of a lower low dressed up as discipline.
